1928 — today

A king's hunting lodge

Built in 1928 by King Alexander I, so that the guests he brought here to hunt would have somewhere to sleep.

The remoteness you came for was the entire point. These forests were royal hunting ground, a long way from anywhere, and a king who wanted to entertain properly needed a house in them.

Nearly a hundred years later the forest is a national park, the hunting parties are gone, and the house does much the same job for a different set of guests.

Through the years

1924

The mountains are protected

Four years before the house was built, the valley of the Triglav lakes over the ridge became a conservation park — one of the earliest protected landscapes in Europe. The idea that this place was worth keeping came first.
1928

The lodge goes up

King Alexander I of Yugoslavia has the house built for his hunting guests. It is deliberately hard to reach, and deliberately comfortable once you are here.
1934

The king is killed

Alexander is assassinated in Marseille. The hunting parties end with him, and the house begins a much quieter century.
1961

Triglav National Park

The protected area is formally made a national park, and later extended to the size it is now — taking in this valley, the plateaus on either side, and the mountains above. The house ends up inside it.
Between

A war, a new country, and then another

What happened to the house in those years is the part I could not find out.

Who lived here after the king? Was it requisitioned, farmed, left empty? Anything you know — even half-remembered village stories — belongs here.
Now

How it came to you

When you took the house on, what you restored, and what you deliberately left exactly as it was. This is the paragraph guests will remember longest.

Where you are

The house stands in the Radovna valley, between the Pokljuka plateau above and Mežakla across the river, inside Triglav National Park — the only national park in Slovenia, named after the mountain at its heart.

Triglav means three heads. It is on the flag, on the coat of arms, and on the wall of half the houses in the country. Slovenes will tell you that you are not properly Slovene until you have climbed it; you can see it from the top of Viševnik, which is a great deal easier.

One or two things about Gorje and this valley that a guest would never find in a guidebook — a story, a custom, something about the neighbours or the seasons.
