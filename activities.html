Within an hour of the house

Days out

A week's worth, in rough order of how close they are.

Lake Bled

  • 10 km
  • Half a day
  • All year

Walk the six kilometres round the shore, take a pletna boat out to the island and climb the ninety-nine steps to the church, then eat cream cake looking back at where you have been. It is busy from mid-morning to late afternoon in summer — so go at eight, or go at six in the evening when the light is better anyway.

Bled Castle

  • 10 km
  • 2 hours
  • Ticket

On a cliff a hundred metres above the water, with the best view of the lake from anywhere you can drive to. Inside there is a printing works, a wine cellar where you bottle and seal your own, and a small museum. Worth the entrance fee for the terrace alone.

Cycling the Radovna

  • From the door
  • Flat
  • Children welcome

The valley road is quiet, paved and almost level — the rare Alpine ride a nine-year-old can do without complaint. Ride up to the top of the valley and freewheel most of the way home.

Pokljuka & the biathlon centre

  • Up the hill
  • Winter & summer

Rudno polje, at the top of the road above the house, is Slovenia's World Cup biathlon venue — and out of season it is simply a beautiful high plateau of spruce forest and pasture. In winter, kilometres of groomed cross-country trails; in summer, the start of every mountain walk worth doing.

Bohinj, Savica and Vogel

  • ~35 km
  • Full day
  • Cable car

Bled's bigger, wilder sister lake — less polished, better for swimming, ringed by mountains. Add the Savica waterfall at the head of the valley, and the Vogel cable car for a view over the whole lake with Triglav behind it. One of the best days in Slovenia.

Radovljica old town

  • 15 km
  • Half a day
  • Rainy-day friendly

A medieval square that somehow escaped the twentieth century, with a beekeeping museum that is far more interesting than it sounds — Slovenia has been serious about bees for three hundred years, and the painted hive fronts are small folk paintings. Then gingerbread, then lunch.

When it rains

  • Indoors
  • Good with children

It does rain here. The thermal pools in Bled are the standard answer — warm water, indoors and out, and a whole afternoon gone. Otherwise: the castle, the beekeeping museum, or the games room, the wood stove and nobody going anywhere.

Where do you actually send people in bad weather? Your answer will be better than mine.

Through the year

Spring — waterfalls at their fullest, the gorge just reopened, meadows full of flowers, snow still on the tops.

Summer — swimming in both lakes, the high walks all open, and long evenings on the terrace. Busy at Bled, never busy here.

Autumn — the larches on Pokljuka turn gold, the crowds go home, and the light is the best of the year.

Winter — cross-country skiing on the plateau, downhill at Vogel or Kranjska Gora, and a wood stove to come back to.
